# decisions r1 §3.4: the legacy fallback

Moved whole from `design/decisions-r1.md` at its r2 fold (2026-09-23), byte for byte, so the record stays under its 40 KB ceiling; its `§` references are that record's sections and its paths resolve as that record's head says (D14).

Configured by `[legacy]` (§2.5); the fixtures are `reference/example-ops/OWNER-DECISIONS.md` (242,404 bytes, 1,643 lines at the pin) and `reference/example-ops/OWNER-DECISIONS-INDEX.md` (41,576 bytes). Nothing is converted; the ledger is read in place, by id.

`show <id> [n]` reads `decisions/` first, then the ledger. The ledger has two entry forms (`research/01-ledger-shape.md:5-25`, re-measured at the fixture):

- **Form A**, a line at column 1 beginning `R<r>-<n>. `, where n may carry a one-letter suffix that makes its own id (`R23-1a` at `reference/example-ops/OWNER-DECISIONS.md:314`, `:325`, `:343`; 151 such lines, the three lettered among them; an indented line that begins with an id is a continuation or a refinement, `:874`, `:883`, `:914`, never an entry; one entry line opens with two ids, `:710`, R15-1 narrowing R13-2, and the entry's id is the first). The entry runs from that line to the line before the next column-1 id line or the next `#` heading, trailing blank lines dropped.
- **Form B**, one line beginning `- **R<r>-<n> (<date>)` (66 lines). The entry is that line; the longest is 13,654 bytes (`reference/example-ops/OWNER-DECISIONS.md:45`, R25-86).

Sub-decisions are `(n)` markers inside the entry. The markers are bold, `**(n)`, in some entries (R25-63 at `reference/example-ops/OWNER-DECISIONS.md:78`, R25-86 at `:45`) and plain in others (R25-87 at `:1641`), and an entry's headline may quote the owner's own numbered answers before the rulings (`:78` opens with the owner's `(1) b` through `(4) a` before its first bold marker). The rule: markers are searched after the headline, which is the first `**...**` span of the entry's first line, Form A or B; bold markers are preferred when the entry has any; a `(n)` that follows an id token, as in `R25-60 (5)`, is a citation, not a marker; sub-decision n is the span from its marker to the next marker or the entry's end. `show <id>` with no n prints the header and one line per marker, `(n)` and its first sentence, and an entry with no markers whole. The header line prints the ledger line number. A missing n exits 1 with `no (n) in <id>`. The packet names the four fixtures a test must pin: R25-63 (4), R25-86 (13), R25-87 (3), and a Form A id, R16-2 (`:671`).

Standing comes from the hand index: the row beginning `| <id> |` and its fifth cell, mapped on the cell's opening words. A cell beginning `IN FORCE` prints `in-force`; beginning `SUPERSEDED by R<r>-<n>` prints `superseded by R<r>-<n>`; beginning `SPENT` prints `spent`; the rest of the cell, a decoration such as `(built)`, follows the status on the same line; any other cell, such as `half SPENT; the deletion half is IN FORCE` (`reference/example-ops/OWNER-DECISIONS-INDEX.md:211`), prints `mixed: <cell>` verbatim and matches no `--status`; no row prints `not indexed`. Measured at the fixture: of 210 R-id rows, 91 open with `IN FORCE`, 88 with `SPENT`, 18 with `SUPERSEDED by`, and 13 are mixed. At the pin the index's last R-id row is R25-85 (`reference/example-ops/OWNER-DECISIONS-INDEX.md:287`) while the ledger runs to R25-89 (`reference/example-ops/OWNER-DECISIONS.md:1642-1643`), so R25-86 to R25-89 print `not indexed`. The index's rows without an R-id (the first round's `1 (A1)` to `21 (F21)` at `reference/example-ops/OWNER-DECISIONS-INDEX.md:11-31`, the six `SEC-` rows at `:37-42`, and the contradiction rows `C1` to `C6` at `:295-300`) are outside the fallback.

`scope --governs X` reads the records, then the index: rows whose fourth cell contains X, case-insensitively, and whose standing maps to `--status` (`superseded by R<r>-<n>` matches `superseded`; a `mixed:` cell matches nothing), printed after the record rows in index order as `<id> legacy <status> <third cell> <file:line>`, the same line under `--full`. The index's `governs` column is free text, so this is a substring match, and the one count line says so whenever a legacy row is present: `# <k> rows (legacy rows by substring over the hand index)`. A legacy row never prints a sub-decision number, because the index has none.
